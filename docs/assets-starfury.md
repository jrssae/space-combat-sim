# `starfury_aurora`: the player's ship (M1-T04, reworked after the M1 play-test)

Generated by `tools/procgen/assets/starfury_aurora.py` from reusable parts (`tools/procgen/parts/`) and the
`earthforce` style (`tools/procgen/styles/earthforce.py`). Everything below refers to the default build
(`seed 42`; surface samples at 0.047 m, the cockpit faces at 0.022 m). Regenerate with `scripts/gen_assets.sh`; outputs land in
`assets/generated/starfury_aurora/` and are never committed.

The M1 build (a long cylindrical fuselage, cylindrical pods at 45 degrees) read as a Thunderbolt. Generator v4
(M3-T26) rebuilds the hull after the maintainer's reference model (`docs/design-references.md`; the model file is not
in the repo): every station, outline and profile below was measured off its silhouettes and cross-sections, and the
geometry is our own lofts and lathes through them. It has a hexagonal glazed beak for a nose, a body slung between two
decks, four arms that are each a deck plate and a brace merging into one slotted beam, pods with a dorsal dome and an
outboard sensor bulb, and a bent pincer and a smaller blade behind every pod. Its silhouettes match the reference's
(intersection over union of the orthographic silhouettes: 0.95 from above, 0.94 from the side, 0.87 from the front).

## Frames

1. **Authored frame**: Godot axes (+X right, +Y up, -Z forward), metres: the reference model's frame, its bounding
   box centred on the origin (the canopy's tip at z = -4.93, the pods' axes at x = +-5.53, y = +-2.295). Parts are
   placed here and read back for anchors.
2. **Symmetric**: the sample set is made *exactly* mirror-symmetric in x (centre-line parts keep their x > 0 samples
   and mirror them; each arm is sampled once and mirrored across x). The pods and claws are also mirrored across
   y = 0; the upper and lower struts are two variants (the lower brace is rooted lower on the fuselage), each
   mirrored across x.
3. **Centred**: the surface samples' mean y and z (about -0.096 m and -0.523 m at seed 42, `manifest.centroid_offset`) are
   subtracted from everything (meshes, hardpoints), so the samples' centroid is (0, 0, 0) to float32 precision. The fuselage hangs low between the arms (chin
   guns, keel box), so the centroid is under the pods' centre line: in the final frame the pods' centre line (the
   thrust axis) is at y = +0.096 and the CoM sits on it. Before v4 only the body was shifted in y, which kept the
   pods centred on y = 0 but here would have lifted the fuselage 0.68 m out of place. The origin moves by
   millimetres between seeds; pod x values and all y and z *differences* are seed-independent.

Overall: 9.83 m long (the keel box's prow to the claw tips), 15.39 m across the claws, 7.47 m tall: the reference's
9.85 x 15.37 x 7.46 m. 48 000 kg (part masses sum to `ShipSpec.mass`; `mass_kg` is informational, `ShipSpec` stays
the authority). Generator v3 (the first pass after the reference) kept the old cockpit and truss struts; v4 rebuilt
everything.

Parts are parametric: a spec dataclass plus a `Style` in, a `Part` (tessellated `Mesh`, named `Anchor`s with
optional directions, `Collision` primitives, mass) out; the asset only places parts and reads anchors afterwards,
so hardpoints, the OBJ and the textured mesh cannot disagree. The Starfury-specific builders live in `parts/starfury.py`; their
specs are tables of measured stations (plate stations, pod profile, blade sections), and `section_loft` lofts any
section with per-edge materials and hard edges.

- `nav_lights` (M3-T27): the running-light markers on the highest point of the upper pods (port on -X, starboard on +X) and on the cabin top

## The textured mesh (M4-T30a, T30b)

The hull is the parts' own meshes, unwrapped into one UV atlas and painted into four maps by `core/textured_mesh.py`
(the contract and the bake are in `docs/architecture.md` §9 and §17.18):

- **Albedo**: the panel lattice and tint of `Style.shade_panels`, plating with rivet rows, markings (stencils, the livery's number
  fields), wear (edge wear, streaks, grime, soot behind the nozzles); the base hull is a weathered blue-grey with no hazard stripes
  (Q37, Q41). The wing livery is a layer in `hull_livery.gdshader` over the shared maps, per ship as instance uniforms.
- **Normal**: panel grooves, hatch bevels and rivets as a height field, at twice the atlas (4 096 px); with `render_triangles` the
  fine hull's shape is baked in too.
- **ORM**: occlusion from a disk-to-point sum over the surface samples, roughness and metal from the materials (matte paint).
- **Emissive**: the canopy's dark red cabin glow (the glass shader scales it by `settings.cabin_glow_level`), the top lamp's lens
  and the main nozzles' throats.
- **Glass**: the window panes are the second primitive, dark and see-through (`hull_glass.gdshader`).

## Surface (M7-T15)

The hull's surface is drawn per part from the maintainer's references (`references/T40 seam references/`: the top, bottom and
three-quarter close-ups and the two paint schemes; never committed, other artists' renders, inspiration only, nothing reused or
traced). The layouts are `layouts.starfury()` in `tools/procgen/parts/layouts.py`, baked as the civilians' are (architecture
§18.30) with the generic staggered plating off. Meshes, chunks, hardpoints, the emissive map and the wing liveries (M4-T30) do
not change: the liveries still paint over the maps per ship, and the allies' Starfuries share this asset and both texture sets.

- **Read, top.** The deck is the busiest face. Each arm's upper deck carries a frame inside its leading and trailing edges
  and two dark channels bent round lighter panels, one inboard and one outboard, with an oblong port between them and a few
  small rectangular hatches. The cabin roof has a spine channel, darker panels either side of it, two round ports side by
  side and wedge panels beside the canopy. Plates are mid-sized (0.4 to 1 m) and differ in grey a little.
- **Read, bottom.** The lower decks are plainer: long strakes swept with the leading edge, a row of slots just behind it,
  short ribs, a framed rectangle or two. The keel is a ladder of sunk pockets down the centre between the guns' fairings.
- **Read, pods and claws.** The pods are ringed: courses of plates round the drum between the grooves, a ribbed rear housing
  over the nozzle, panels of mixed length. The claws are toothed along their length.
- **High set (`texture_detail = high`, the default; the maintainer's option A, 2026-11-13).** Upper decks (`strut_ur`, its
  twin `strut_ul`): the frame, the two bent channels and the oblong port, small hatches, plates 0.9 by 0.7 m split down to
  0.2 m in 6 % tone. Lower decks (`strut_lr`, `strut_ll`, seen from below): three strakes parallel to the leading edge, four
  slots behind it, two framed rectangles, cabin rings, plates 1.5 by 0.65 m swept with the edge in 4.5 % tone. Both carry the
  cabin roof's spine, panels and ports. Pods: ring courses of plates of mixed length (7 % tone) between the grooves and a
  ribbed rear housing (twelve ribs). Fuselage: the flank belt, two rings, the keel's ladder of sunk pockets (two rows of
  five), a hatch on each flank, plates in 6 % tone. Claws: rings of teeth every 0.2 m (unchanged from the references' blades).
- **Low set (`texture_detail = low`: major seams only).** The frame inside each deck's edges, the joints where the arm leaves
  the cabin (x 1.97 m) and at mid span (x 3.4 m), the cabin's two frames (z -2.95 and -1.45 m), and the pod's two grooves.
  Written as `starfury_aurora_{albedo,normal,orm}_low.png`, named in every surface's `detail.low`; the glow map is shared.
- **Sheet.** `references/screenshots/M7-T15 surface sheets/M7-T15_options_starfury.png` (the choice: current, low, A, B, C;
  RX 6950 XT, Forward+) and the as-built low and high frames under `final/`. Generator version 10.

## Break-up chunks (M3-T03)

`core/chunks.py` groups the parts into `cabin` (fuselage, cockpit faces, guns, fittings) and `arm_ur`, `arm_lr`, `arm_ul`,
`arm_ll` (each a pod, its strut and its claw). Each chunk is placed at its parts' surface samples' centroid (`offset`, body frame),
its OBJ re-centred there, and its textured mesh cut from the hull's on the hull's UVs (M4-T30d), so a chunk draws with the hull's
materials and wears its ship's livery. `hardpoints.json` `chunks[]` is `{name, obj, offset, joint, mesh}`; `joint` is where the
chunk leaves the body in the staged break-up (M3-T28).

## Determinism and options

Everything comes from `ctx.rng("surface", <part>)` streams (one per part) and pure geometry; two builds with the same seed are
byte-identical (checked by SHA-256 in `test_starfury_aurora.py`). Options (`BuildContext.options`, CLI `--option`): `spacing` (m,
0.047; the surface samples'), `cockpit_spacing` (m, 0.47 x spacing), `render_triangles` (0: the full hull), `atlas_px`, `texel_m`,
`occlusion`.

## Known limits / for the human tester `(H)`

- Judge the cockpit view against the maintainer's reference (frame weight, sill height, green tint), the
  silhouette in chase and orbit views, and the claws' read from the front.
- Simplifications against the reference: the emblem relief on the rear panel, the pipework round the guns, the
  small brackets and vents are not modelled; the deck's surface is flat where the reference has shallow facets; the
  claw prongs end straight where the reference's hook in slightly. The reference has no colours: the glazing (every
  facet of the beak, so the pilot can see ahead), the dark recesses and the hazard bands are our choices.
