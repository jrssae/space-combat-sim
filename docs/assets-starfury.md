# `starfury_aurora`: the player's ship (M1-T04, reworked after the M1 play-test)

Generated by `tools/procgen/assets/starfury_aurora.py` from reusable parts (`tools/procgen/parts/`) and the
`earthforce` style (`tools/procgen/styles/earthforce.py`). Everything below refers to the default build
(`seed 42`, spacing 0.047 m, cockpit spacing 0.022 m). Regenerate with `scripts/gen_assets.sh`; outputs land in
`assets/generated/starfury_aurora/` and are never committed.

The M1 build (a long cylindrical fuselage, cylindrical pods at 45 degrees) read as a Thunderbolt. Generator v4
(M3-T26) rebuilds the hull after the maintainer's reference model (`docs/design-references.md`; the model file is not
in the repo): every station, outline and profile below was measured off its silhouettes and cross-sections, and the
geometry is our own lofts and lathes through them. It has a hexagonal glazed beak for a nose, a body slung between two
decks, four arms that are each a deck plate and a brace merging into one slotted beam, pods with a dorsal dome and an
outboard sensor bulb, and a bent pincer and a smaller blade behind every pod. Its silhouettes match the reference's
(intersection over union of the orthographic silhouettes: 0.95 from above, 0.94 from the side, 0.87 from the front).

## Frames

1. **Authored frame**: Godot axes (+X right, +Y up, -Z forward), metres: the reference model's frame, its bounding
   box centred on the origin (the canopy's tip at z = -4.93, the pods' axes at x = +-5.53, y = +-2.295). Parts are
   placed here and read back for anchors.
2. **Symmetric**: the sample set is made *exactly* mirror-symmetric in x (centre-line parts keep their x > 0 samples
   and mirror them; each arm is sampled once and mirrored across x). The pods and claws are also mirrored across
   y = 0; the upper and lower struts are two variants (the lower brace is rooted lower on the fuselage), each
   mirrored across x.
3. **Centred**: the sample mean y and z (-0.096 m and -0.523 m at seed 42, `manifest.centroid_offset`) are
   subtracted from everything (splats, meshes, hardpoints), so the written splat centroid is (0, 0, 0) to float32
   precision and GDGS's unconditional import centring is a no-op. The fuselage hangs low between the arms (chin
   guns, keel box), so the centroid is under the pods' centre line: in the final frame the pods' centre line (the
   thrust axis) is at y = +0.096 and the CoM sits on it. Before v4 only the body was shifted in y, which kept the
   pods centred on y = 0 but here would have lifted the fuselage 0.68 m out of place. The origin moves by
   millimetres between seeds; pod x values and all y and z *differences* are seed-independent.
4. **PLY only**: positions, normals and rotations are pre-rotated by Rz(180 deg) (`core/frames.py`) so the
   `GaussianSplatNode` stays at identity. Meshes and hardpoints stay in the Godot frame.

## Cockpit view

The camera sits inside the glazed beak, 1.08 m behind its tip and 0.7 m above the pods' centre plane
(`cockpit_camera`, looking -Z). Seen from there (75 deg vertical FOV):

- the view ahead is through the beak's top and upper side panes; the frame bars run from the corners of the full
  section in to the small front pane at the tip, which sits 25 deg below the line of sight;
- the deck's leading edge overhangs the canopy above, its cheeks at the upper left and right; the flank port blocks
  show through the side panes;
- nothing crosses the centre: no opaque splat (soft edge, 2.5 sigma, included) and no cockpit-mesh vertex lies in
  the box |azimuth| < 20 deg, -12 deg < elevation < 28 deg (tested), and the guns are 70 deg down, under the chin.

The cockpit mesh is depth-tested against the splats, so its bars hide the frame splats behind them; the hull depth
proxy (`HullDepthProxy`, back-face culled) does not block the window from inside. The targeting grid of the
reference is a HUD element (M2-T08) and is not modelled. `(H)`: compare the cockpit screenshot with the reference.

## Splats: how the hull is sampled and why

The top rendering risk is a "dotty" hull in the cockpit view (`docs/architecture.md` section 7.4). The choices
below are what keeps the surface continuous; `core/sampling.py` has the details.

- **Area-weighted, stratified.** Each face gets `area / spacing^2` samples in expectation; the fractional part is
  rounded stochastically (`floor(expected + u)`) so the expectation is exact and tiny faces are not all dropped.
- **Low discrepancy inside faces.** Points come from the R2 quasi-random sequence mapped to each triangle with
  Heitz's low-distortion square-to-triangle map, with a random offset per face (nearest-neighbour spacing: median
  0.57 s, 1st percentile 0.2 s; a random pattern gives 0.45 s with many clumps and holes), so the overlap needed to
  close the sheet is small and predictable.
- **Flat, oriented Gaussians.** Rotation from the interpolated vertex normal (the hull is faceted, so mostly hard
  edges), in-plane sigma `k s` with `k = 1.4`, normal sigma `0.03 s`: thin discs that blend into a sheet. Measured
  composite opacity of the sheet at independent test points (alpha 0.98, worst 0.1 percentile / minimum): k = 0.8
  -> 0.77 / 0.46, k = 1.0 -> 0.98 / 0.89, k = 1.2 -> 0.999 / 0.994, **k = 1.4 -> 1.000 / 0.9999**. k = 1.4 stays
  the default; it blurs edges by about 2 sigma. `--option overlap=1.2` is the first thing to try for crisper lines.
- **Spacing 0.047 m, cockpit 0.022 m.** The rebuilt hull (v4) has about a third more surface than v3; at 0.04 m it
  came to 330k splats, over the AC3 budget of 100k-250k, so the hull spacing went to 0.047 m (244k) with k unchanged.
  Samples buried inside another part are dropped (strut roots in the fuselage, strut tips and claw roots in the pods,
  the bells' outer walls, the knuckle columns' bases), and so are samples whose face presses against another part
  (their normal runs into it within 5 cm: the deck's underside on the fuselage, the fittings against the flanks).
  The faces the pilot sees from 0.6-1 m (frames, chin, lining of the window segment; `hull.wedge_cockpit_faces`)
  are sampled at 0.47 s = 0.022 m as before: a splat's soft edge (2.5 sigma) spans about 5 degrees there instead of
  10, which keeps the frames thin and the centre clear. `--spacing` and `--option cockpit_spacing=...` override
  both; the manifest records them.
- **Baked studio light** (`core/lighting.py`): hemisphere ambient plus a warm key from upper front right, a cool
  fill from lower left rear and a rim from above and behind; no view-dependent terms. Lighting rotates with the
  ship; a real sun needs GDGS relighting later (section 7.3).
- **Surface detail** (`styles/`): paneled materials get a world-aligned 0.6 m panel grid with 4 cm panel lines and a
  +-5 % per-panel tint; the hazard bands, the ribbed pod housings (`hull_panel` / `hull_dark`) and the dark tail are
  materials on the geometry, so they also appear in the OBJ.
- **Glass.** The camera looks out through the panes, so they are a `transmittance` 0.7 material: per-splat alpha
  `1 - T^(1 / (2 pi k^2))` (about 0.029), dark green, so the sheet lets 70 % through with a faint green tint. From
  outside they read as green-tinted glass over the dark lining and bulkhead; the frames are opaque. GDGS shrinks a
  splat's footprint by `alpha^0.2`, so the glass is cheap to sort.

## LOD1 and break-up chunks (M3-T03)

Allies use a lighter Starfury and every Starfury break-up uses chunks (`docs/architecture.md` §16 D35). Both come
from one extra sampling pass; LOD0's files do not change (when T03 landed, the PLY, both OBJs and both MTLs were
byte-identical to the pre-M3 build; `test_starfury_aurora.py` pins the PLY's counts, header and sample splat records,
re-pinned for generator v3 and v4).

- **Resampling.** `build_parts` runs a second time with every spacing times `LOD1_SPACING_FACTOR` = 2.0 (hull
  0.094 m, cockpit faces 0.044 m) and every RNG stream suffixed `lod1` (`ctx.rng("splats", <part>, "lod1")`), so
  LOD0's streams are never touched. Splat scales grow with the spacing (`to_splats`: in-plane sigma 0.094 m, normal
  sigma 7.5 mm), so the sheet closes the way LOD0's does. Same parts, burial rules, materials, glass and baked light.
- **Count.** 60 956 splats, 25.0 % of LOD0's 243 728 (AC6 range 20-30 %, checked by a build warning and the tests).
  Manifest `lods[1]`: `{"level": 1, "file": "starfury_aurora_lod1.ply", "splats": 60956, "spacing_m": 0.094}`.
- **Frame.** LOD1 is centred like LOD0 (its own y and z mean), so its splat centroid is the origin and GDGS's
  import centring is a no-op for it too. Its frame differs from LOD0's (the hardpoints frame) by the difference of
  the two means: 2.0 mm in y and 1.7 mm in z at seed 42. Its AABB is LOD0's within 1.1 cm (AC6 allows 0.3 m). Exact
  x symmetry holds as for LOD0.
- **Coverage.** Composite opacity at 12 000 independent probe points on the visible hull (resampled from `probe`
  streams; glass left out): LOD0 mean 0.988 with 0.35 % of points under 0.5, LOD1 mean 0.983 with 0.52 % under 0.5.
  The points under 0.5 sit on seams where one part runs into another (the keel box into the fuselage, struts into
  the pods): a probe there sees half a sheet. On the fuselage, struts and pods LOD1 stays above 0.97 on average; what
  thins out are features smaller than a 9 cm splat (the clamps, rails, RCS blocks and cones, ring edges, frame bands).
- **Chunks** (`core/chunks.py`, as for the raider). LOD1 is written chunk by chunk, so the chunks partition it
  exactly: `cabin` (fuselage, cockpit faces, guns, fittings; 9 692 splats) and `arm_ur`, `arm_lr`, `arm_ul`,
  `arm_ll` (each a pod, its strut and its claw; 12 988 splats each for the upper arms, 12 644 for the lower), in that
  order. The arm suffixes are the pod names;
  `DamageZones`' X-fighter zones are the cabin and the four arms but carry no string names, so these names stand.
  `hardpoints.json` gains `chunks[]` `{name, ply, obj, offset, splats}` as its last key; each chunk PLY and OBJ is
  re-centred on its own splat centroid, and `offset` is that centroid in the body frame (LOD1's frame). The chunk OBJ
  is the same part meshes' fallback mesh (the cockpit interior is not in any chunk): the five OBJs back at their
  offsets are the hull OBJ, vertex for vertex in count, within 1 cm in bounds. The manifest gains `chunks` (the
  names) and `chunk_lod` 1 (the LOD the chunks partition; absent means 0, as for the raider).
- **Unchanged by T03.** `generator_version` stayed 2 (it is in the LOD0 PLY header), and so did the cockpit
  interior, the hull depth proxy (the LOD0 fallback mesh) and every LOD0 hardpoint.

Until M3-T08 prepares Starfury chunk sets in `DebrisPool`, the player's death already uses these chunks, as
mesh-only debris (the DestructionDirector bursts any `chunks[]` it finds; `test_destruction.gd` expects that).

## Determinism and options

Everything comes from `ctx.rng("splats", <part>)` streams (one per part; LOD1's are suffixed `lod1`) and pure
geometry; two builds with the same seed are byte-identical (checked by SHA-256 in `test_starfury_aurora.py`). Options
(`BuildContext.options`, CLI `--spacing` / `--option`): `spacing` (m, 0.047), `cockpit_spacing` (m, 0.47 x spacing),
`overlap` (k, 1.0 since the post-M2 play test), `thickness` (normal sigma / s, 0.08), `lod1_factor` (LOD1 spacing /
LOD0 spacing, 2.0; LOD0 is byte-identical whatever its value). The AC3 budget warning only fires at the default
spacing, the AC6 LOD1 share warning only at the default spacing and factor.

## Known limits / for the human tester `(H)`

- Judge the cockpit view against the maintainer's reference (frame weight, sill height, green tint), the
  silhouette in chase and orbit views, and the claws' read from the front.
- LOD1 (AC18): judge allies at combat range and in formation. At 0.094 m spacing the 4 cm panel lines are sampled
  sparsely and can read speckled up close, edges blur about twice as far as LOD0's, and the small claw details thin
  out (see "Coverage" above).
- No ambient occlusion in the bake: the strut roots and claw blocks can look flat.
- Simplifications against the reference: the emblem relief on the rear panel, the pipework round the guns, the
  small brackets and vents are not modelled; the deck's surface is flat where the reference has shallow facets; the
  claw prongs end straight where the reference's hook in slightly. The reference has no colours: the glazing (every
  facet of the beak, so the pilot can see ahead), the dark recesses and the hazard bands are our choices.
