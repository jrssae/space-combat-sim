# Design references (written descriptions)

The maintainer shared reference images (fan renders and blueprints of Babylon 5 designs). They are copyrighted
third-party art, so **they are not committed**. This page records what matters for our procedural generators, in
our own words. All geometry is generated by `tools/procgen`; nothing is traced or copied from the images.
If a generator needs more detail than this page gives, **ask the maintainer for references**; don't guess.

Proportions are approximate, read off orthographic views. Godot frame for ships: +X right, +Y up, -Z forward.

## SA-23E Starfury (player fighter, M1)

Overall: a compact central fuselage with four long, strongly swept struts carrying four engine pods at the rear
corners. Front-on it is an X; from above it looks like a swept "W" or batwing. Roughly 9-10 m long, about 13 m
wide across the pods and 8 m tall. The X is wider than tall: struts sit about 30-35° above and below horizontal.

- **Fuselage.** From above, an arrowhead or elongated diamond: a pointed nose that widens to a broad,
  squared-off tail about a third of the span wide. From the side, a wedge: thin at the nose, deep at the rear.
  From the front, a shield or heater-badge shape. It is short compared with the span, about 55 % of the overall
  length.
- **Cockpit.** At the front top of the fuselage. A small faceted window (green-tinted glass) faces forward and
  up; the pilot stands upright behind it. From inside, the forward view must be clear: frame members only at the
  edges, nothing across the middle.
- **Struts.** Four flat, wide beams from the rear half of the fuselage sides, swept back hard (about 45-55° from
  the centreline in plan view) and angled up/down into the X. They are trussed: open lattice with rectangular
  cut-outs and a slight bend. Hazard striping (yellow/black or grey/yellow) runs along them.
- **Engine pods.** At the strut tips, level with or slightly behind the fuselage tail. Each is a short, chunky
  cylinder along the flight axis: a rounded front with a ring-framed intake/thruster face visible from the
  front, and the main engine nozzle at the rear.
- **"Claws".** Behind or outboard of each pod is a cluster of two or three angular vanes, the manoeuvring
  thrusters. They splay aft and outward like pincers and are a signature silhouette element in every view
  (front view: they stick out diagonally past the pods).
- **Guns.** Twin pulse-cannon barrels in a mount under the front of the fuselage.
- **Cockpit view (maintainer reference, a frame from the show).** The pilot looks out through a wide forward
  window set in a heavy, dark, faceted frame. A horizontal bar runs across the top. Angled struts run from the
  upper corners inward and down, so the main pane is a broad trapezoid, with narrower side panes visible at the left
  and right edges. A dark sill or console runs below the window. Lower centre, a translucent **targeting display**
  floats in front of the view: a square grid (about 8x8 cells) with a circular reticle and a diagonal marker,
  reddish-orange in the show. The rest of the view is open space; the frame never crosses the centre.
  Implications: `cockpit_interior` is the frame, struts and sill only. The targeting display is a HUD element
  (M2-T08), drawn in the green line theme per the maintainer's HUD answer.
- **Cockpit view, second reference (maintainer, 2026-11-01, fan art, supersedes the frame above for the interior; the image is kept in the
  ignored `references/` directory and never committed).** Two heavy, deep struts rise from the centre console and lean outward to the upper
  corners, forming a V, with a dark bar across the top and dark curved conduits at the top corners. Their inner faces glow saturated red, with
  cable runs along them. The main opening is the V: narrow at the console, wide at the top. Beyond each strut a large side pane shows
  open space. Low on each side a broad sill of dark ribbed panels runs toward the pilot, with a small round white lamp set in each. In the
  lower centre a flat octagonal display sits in the console, rim lit, tilted up to the pilot, showing a red grid with green ship icons and
  a green target ring; a short row of small red and white indicator lamps sits on the console's edge just above it. Everything else is near black, lit
  only by the red. Proportions, at 4:3: the octagon covers the middle quarter of the width and the lowest third of the height.
  Implications: see M3-T29. The show-frame targeting grid above leaves the cockpit view for the octagon.
  As built (M3-T29), the maintainer's slimmer proportions win over this image, for the view: the struts are about half as wide (about 180 px at the top of a
  1080p frame, 100 px at the octagon) and land on the sides of the octagon, with no console above it, so the opening runs down to the octagon's top edge; the
  bar across the top is 15 px; the sills stand 75 px tall at the frame's bottom outside the screens, each lamp inside that; the indicator row is a thin line of
  lamps on the octagon's top rim, showing assist, guns, running lights and auto-track. No conduits were built at the top corners, to keep those corners open.
- **Colours.** Light grey hull with darker panel lines and greebles; hazard stripes on the struts; dark metal
  pods with lighter bands.

- **Reference model (2026-10-30).** The maintainer added a fan-made 3D-print model of the Starfury (an STL in the
  untracked `references/`, never committed). Its geometry is not copied: we read orthographic renders and
  cross-sections of it, scaled to a 15.4 m claw span, and rebuilt the hull from those measurements.
  - **Overall**: 9.85 m long, 15.4 m across the claws, 7.5 m tall.
  - **Nose**: a hexagonal beak. Its blunt tip is a small hexagon at mid height; flat facets flare back over about
    1 m to the full section (about 1.5 m wide, 2.1 m deep). The deck overhangs it from above, with cheeks either
    side reaching further forward.
  - **Body**: slung between two decks. The upper deck runs across the top from one upper arm to the other, the lower
    deck the same underneath. Under the chin: a keel box along the centre line, and on each side a long gun fairing
    ending in a block with a heavy barrel over a lighter one. On each flank: a block with two forward ports under the
    deck, a pointed teardrop fairing lower down, and a wedge fairing between the upper flank and the deck. The rear
    is a hexagonal panel.
  - **Arms**: each is two plates. The deck plate climbs from the body out to the pod. A brace plate starts at the
    body (at the shoulder for the upper arms, low on the flank for the lower ones) and closes in under the deck
    plate. From about 3 m out they run as one thick beam, with a slot and ladder bays along both edges. Seen from
    the front they rise about 23 degrees. The leading edge is swept about 33 degrees beyond a steeper inboard
    section; the trailing edge has a tooth.
  - **Pods**: 5.6 m lathes on axes at (±5.53, ±2.3). Front to back: a narrow ring face, a groove, a long body
    widening to a 0.93 m radius, a second groove, then a rear housing tapering to the main nozzle's face. Ahead of
    the middle, a dome stands up (down on the lower pods) and a sensor bulb out, both 1.44 m from the axis. Two rings
    of clamps.
  - **Claws**: behind each pod, a plate bent along its length over the pod's outboard flank. It splits about 3 m
    behind the pod centre into an upper and a lower prong, which reach a further 1.7 m. A smaller bent blade sits
    under the arm on the inboard side.

  `starfury_aurora` generator v4 (M3-T26) is built from these measurements (`docs/assets-starfury.md`).

The M1 generator produced a long cylindrical fuselage with cylindrical pods. The maintainer judged that
closer to a Thunderbolt than a Starfury. The rework must hit the silhouette above.

## Babylon 5 station (M7)

Refined in M7-T01 from the maintainer's references (Q1): the STL `references/EA Babylon 5 Station.stl` for the proportions,
two blueprints (Andy Crook's schematic, Lawrence Miller's lettered sheets), a show still (`babylon 5 station colors.jpg`) and
fan close-ups for colour, surfaces and lights. The numbers, the source list and the colour table are in
`docs/assets-station.md`; this is the description in words.

8 km long along one axis, about 830 m across the drum's widest bands. Two long booms beside the aft drum carry twelve
vane blades that reach 1.5 km above and below the axis, so the station is 3 km tall and 1.1 km wide overall. Two
structures share the axis. A **turning drum** runs from the docking sphere at the bow to the aft band, a little under
5 km. A **stationary frame** holds it from behind (hub, booms, reactor) and from above, where one long dorsal spine runs
from the stern over the whole drum to the bow spike.

From the bow to the stern:

1. **Bow spike.** A flat fork on the dorsal spine's front end, two prongs with a slot between them, about 560 m long. A
   pylon drops from it to the docking aperture's outer ring.
2. **Docking aperture.** A toothed ring about 300 m across on the axis, a cross-shaped four-leaf door inside it.
3. **Docking sphere** (the Blue section's command sphere). A sphere about 550 m across, blue and grey checkerboard.
4. **Cobra-bay arms.** Four long arms at 90 degree spacing (top, both sides, bottom) on the sphere and the waist behind it,
   each with a broad head on the sphere, two forward prongs, and a narrower 94 m wide section aft. A row of seven numbered,
   hazard-striped hatches on the outward face; red stripes along the edges.
5. **Forward body, ring and forward band.** A barrel about 660 m across, a toothed ring, then the first wide band (about
   830 m across, 550 m long).
6. **Green zone.** The long middle of the drum, about 660 m across and 2.5 km long, with a slightly wider belt at mid
   length and raised stringers along it.
7. **Aft band.** A second wide band like the forward one, rounded at both shoulders.
8. **Hub.** Behind a narrow neck (the drum's aft bearing): a short drum, then a cone tapering to the truss, with two flat
   delta fins out to the sides.
9. **Vane booms and vanes.** Two thin rails at the hub's sides running forward beside the aft drum, about 120 m clear of
   it. Each carries three tall vanes ("ionization vanes"), each a pair of blades, one up and one down: thin deep-blue
   plates in a grey frame, widest near the root and tapering to a notched tip. In profile they stand above and below like
   a comb; front on they are long thin vertical lines at the sides (there are no separate antenna masts).
10. **Truss spine.** An open box truss about 210 m across and 760 m long.
11. **Torus disc.** A disc about 510 m across with radial ribs and toothed rim.
12. **Shaft and reactor.** A slim shaft with machinery bays, then the reactor drum (about 340 m across), its aft face a
    ring of bulging pipe sections round a flat disc.

**The dorsal spine** (the Brown section) is the frame's backbone: warm grey-brown, faceted, 9 m above the drum's wide
bands and about 90 m above the green zone. It carries the observation dome near the bow, two ribbed radiator blocks over
the wide bands and a row of six red cargo modules over the green zone.

**What turns.** The references (Miller's Blue section sheet: rotation compensators, scanners matching the station's
rotation, elevators from the Blue section to the Green zone; the support spine the rotation section can be jettisoned
from) put the docking sphere, the Cobra arms and the whole drum on the turning part. That moves the Cobra bays from the
stationary command section, as this page read before T01, onto the drum. The maintainer confirms it (Q3).

**Defence grid.** Its emplacements are settled with the maintainer in T02; `docs/assets-station.md` lists where the
references show turret-like features.

**Colours.** The drum light grey with large blue-violet panels in stepped blocks; the vanes deep blue; the spine, the bow
spike and the hub warm grey-brown; the reactor end darker bronze grey; the cargo modules dull red; red trim stripes on the
arms and the bow spine, hazard yellow on the bay hatches.

**Lights.** A red lamp at the observation dome, red lit panels on the bow spine, yellow strip lights on the arms; windows
are not readable in the captures and come from Q10.

## Epsilon III (M6)

The planet Babylon 5 orbits. No moons. 0.75 g at the surface, about 9 560 km across (`docs/milestones/m6-epsilon-space-at-true-scale.md`,
Q1). Reference: one show still of the planet in sunlight (`references/Epsilon III planet.png`, maintainer, 2026-11-10).

- **Colour.** Warm and dry: rust, tan and ochre. Darker brown lowlands, lighter beige-cream highlands and plateaus,
  some pale patches near the top that read as high ground or haze rather than ice. No blue oceans; no visible water.
- **Surface.** Strong relief at every scale: wrinkled ridges, rough mottled terrain and canyon-like grooves, so the lit
  side looks bumpy, not smooth. Lit areas show fine grain from the relief catching the light.
- **Clouds.** Not distinct cloud decks; at most thin pale wisps over the brighter areas. The surface dominates.
- **Light.** A hard terminator: lit from the upper right, with the lower left in deep shadow that fades to near black.
  No city lights on the night side.
- **Limb.** A faint warm orange haze along the lit edge only; no blue atmospheric ring. A thin atmosphere at most.
- **Setting.** Black space with dense small stars and a blue nebula wash behind (that is the sky, not part of the planet).

## Jumpgate (M6)

The Earth Alliance gate ships use to enter and leave hyperspace (M6-T13, D69, Q11). The shape and proportions come from the
maintainer's model `references/EA_Jump_Gate.stl`; the colours, lights and the vortex from show stills and the reference video (sources
below). Nothing from either is copied: `tools/procgen/assets/jumpgate.py` builds its own geometry from the ratios here.

**Layout** (corrected under M6-T19 by the maintainer, 2026-11-11; T13 had laid the arms out as flat spokes). Four identical,
free-standing arms, **parallel to each other and to the vortex's axis**, 90° apart round it at the **top, bottom and both sides**
of the funnel. The side arms stand further from the axis than the top and bottom ones (round 3, 2026-11-11), so the four frame an
**elliptical mouth, wider than tall**. Nothing joins them; the vortex forms inside them: its mouth at the arms' front ends, its funnel running back along them to
their back ends. Seen face on the gate is four dots round the vortex; seen obliquely, four long rails round it (the stills). The
model holds the four arms as separate print pieces, not assembled, so the spacing is read off the stills: the mouth is about as wide
as an arm is long, so the top and bottom arms stand two thirds of an arm out from the axis. **The ellipse's aspect is 1.55**
(generator option `mouth_aspect`; 1.35 in round 3), chosen from `jumpgate.png` (T19 round 4): the camera, the aspect and the
vortex's visible edge were fitted together to the four rails' ends, the vortex's outline and its throat in the still. The
still's vortex reads 1.60 to 1 on screen, which 1.55 gives; the rails' misfit hardly changes between 1.35 and 1.6 (about 26 px
rms in the 385 px still: the still's gate is planar spokes, which parallel rails only approach).

**One arm** (ratios to the arm's length; the model's arm is 164 of its units long):
- **Truss.** A central spine tube (diameter 1.3 %) inside four slim longerons (diameter 0.55 %) on a square 4.9 % across, two on the
  tube's sides and one each toward and away from the axis. Clamp rings sit on the longerons midway between stations and near both ends.
- **Five stations**, evenly spaced 17.4 % apart about the arm's middle (the end ones about 15 % from each end). Each is a pair of
  cross-shaped collars (6 % across, 0.85 % thick, 4.1 % apart) with conical sleeves on the spine between and outside them.
- **Fins.** Each station carries two flat fins, spanning round the tube one each side of the arm: a stretched hexagon, root chord 3 %, widest
  (5.8 %) a fifth of the way out, tip chord 2.7 %, reaching 11.8 % from the arm's axis, 0.2 % thick, with a raised rim round a darker
  inset panel. The pair is not flat but a shallow V of 140°: each fin is tilted 20°, so seen down the arm they form a wide dish.
  Ten fins per arm. The stills show the fins standing out sideways from each rail, round the tube rather than away from the axis,
  so they span tangentially, the V opening toward the axis (a dish facing the funnel).
- **End caps.** A cross-shaped cap at each end, as wide as the collars, its four limbs drooping towards the arm's middle like a hipped
  roof, with a cone and band below it down to the spine.
- Overall cross-section about 6 % of the length across the truss and 22 % across the fin tips; the arm is long and spindly, like a
  girder with feathers.

**Scale.** No on-screen figure was found. A fan "unofficial technical manual" says large permanent gates have "four seven-mile long
generators" (11 km each, larger than the station); the stills do not support that, so it is not used. Instead the vortex must take a
capital ship with room to spare: **arms of 1 200 m**, the top and bottom ones 800 m from the axis and the side ones 1 240 m, a
vortex mouth about 2 400 m wide, 1 510 m tall and 1 200 m deep, the gate about 2.6 km wide, 1.7 km tall and 1.2 km long. The truss is then about 75 m across, a fin about 120 m long, a station pitch about 210 m; a
Starfury is a speck beside a fin. The arm length is one option (`arm_length_m`), everything else follows from the ratios.

**Colours and finish.** Industrial and dark. The truss and spine read warm grey to dull tan (a brassy sheen where lit), the collars and
caps gunmetal, the fins a paler cool grey with darker slate-blue inset panels. Lit from one side the arms show bright edges against
black; in shadow they are near-black silhouettes picked out only by their lights.

**Lights.** A row of small warm lamps along each arm, one at each fin station, steady amber to pale yellow-white; they are what you see
of a gate in shadow. While the vortex forms, the arms' **ends** glow first (the back ends in the game, after the lamps' front-to-back chase): a hot pink-white bloom with spiky rays, the four
glows then merging into one flash in the centre.

**The vortex** (the maintainer's description, 2026-11-11, from T14's screenshots). One effect for every jump point: a gate's,
a capital ship's own (M12) and the open points raiders arrive through.
- **Fit at a gate.** The mouth lies at the arms' front ends, an ellipse whose rim comes **within 1 m of the arms** (semi-axes
  1 198 m across and 754 m up, the trusses reaching about 45 m in from the arms' axes). The funnel runs back along the arms and
  ends at their back ends: as deep as an arm is long (1 200 m). The numbers come from the gate's markers, not constants. A
  ship-made point has no arms to shape it and stays round.
- **The visible body** (round 5, the maintainer): the bright funnel comes right up to the arms, its short feathered edge gone
  by the rim (round 4's body ending at two thirds of the radius was rejected: the opening sat too far from the arms).
- **Shape.** A slight funnel: visible, not pronounced. A broad well with gently curving walls and a wide, rounded base, elliptical
  in section from the mouth down to the throat (the still's dark eye is a wide ellipse too); the dark
  throat a soft smudge, not a hard hole. The dark circle lies on the funnel's base itself, its narrow deep end (round 5,
  the maintainer); from an oblique view the funnel's nearer walls hide part or all of it. The walls are translucent: stars and the gate's far arms partly show through them.
- **Colour and motion.** Layers of colour: the base colour (orange to golden yellow for a departure, a **dark sky blue** for an
  arrival: dark against space but not navy, round 3), darker toward the throat and the rim and brightest over a band in between,
  in broad uneven clouds; soft-edged, roughly rectangular patches darker than the base and paler translucent patches of the base
  colour lying along one soft spiral; and over them **fine, hair-like streaks** combed along that spiral, short and many, as the
  still shows, not separate bold bands. **A departure swirls in** (the pattern travels down toward the throat, drawing ships in)
  and **an arrival swirls out** (up from the throat toward the rim). **Seen from in front of the mouth an arrival turns
  counter-clockwise and a departure clockwise** (round 3); both follow the same spiral, one outward and one inward. The body's
  edge fades out like the still's (round 6, measured): a wide soft fade, about a quarter of the radius from 90 % down to 10 %
  of the body's brightness, broken up into wisps along the spiral, its faint end at the arms.
- **The base by view.** Only the narrow deep end changes with the view: from in front, looking down the funnel, it fades to
  black; from the side or from behind it shows a moderately bright white glow, local to the base, with the rest of the funnel
  visible round it as usual.
- **Opening.** At a gate, first the gate's own activation: the lamps at the fin stations flash one after another along each
  arm from its front end to its back end, all four arms together, and the back ends glow (a hot pink-white bloom with spiky rays);
  then the jump point forms inside the arms. Then, for every jump point,
  the initiation flash at its centre; the funnel then grows out of that point and unfolds toward the rim of its mouth (it does
  not scale up in place). It stays open while ships pass, then folds back and is gone within about a second. A ship-made point
  starts at the flash and goes straight on into the funnel's growth, with no pause (a pause in one reference gif is a fault in
  the gif, the maintainer, 2026-11-11). Ships coming out of an open point appear in its flash.
- **Sizes.** A gate's vortex is fitted to the gate. An open point is sized for the ship coming through, with the gate's
  proportions, and widens when the rest of its group comes out of it.
- **Timing** from the reference video's frames (about one per second): the activation about 1 s, the flash about 1 s, the
  funnel full size 1 to 2 s later; open while the ship passes (3 to 4 s in the clips), the ship a dark speck deep in the eye
  flying out. Ships must pass soon after it opens: the closing vortex is dangerous (the wiki, "backwash"). Only one direction
  per opening; a gate is not shut down quickly.
- **The references in `references/jumpgate/`** (not committed): `jumpgate.png` (the acceptance view: the open blue vortex framed
  by the arms, dim deep blue with paler feathered streaks spiralling to a dark throat), `jumpgate_open` (the tips' activation
  glow), `gathering` (the gate in shadow, its amber lamps), `point_1` (a ship-made point: a mid-blue funnel packed with soft
  darker and paler patches, black eye, wispy rim), `point_2` (the initiation flash: a white core with lavender-blue rays), and
  the video frames (the whole sequence, and the orange-gold departure). `jumpgate.png` is dimmer and deeper blue than the
  others; the game follows the brighter video colours, a little dimmed.

**What the generator gives the game (T14, T19).** The gate's origin is the centre of the arms' front ends, where the vortex's mouth
lies; the gate faces -Z (arrivals leave along -Z, departures fly in along +Z) and the arms run back to z = 1 200 m. `hardpoints.json`
and the manifest carry the vortex (mouth centre, normal, up, rim radius measured a few metres inside the arms, depth = the arm's
length, the colours by direction in linear RGB with the streak highlight, the opening flash colour), the arrival and departure points
(each naming its vortex colour), the four emitters on the arms' back ends, the 40 lamps (each with its station, listed front to back),
the arms' front and back ends and one collision box per truss and per fin. The gate broadcasts to
1 000 km (D69; the wiki and the fan manual both say about 1 000 km for a gate's beacon).

## Raider fighter "Delta-V" (M2)

A flying wing. A triangular delta with a leading-edge sweep of about 60° and a span of about twice the length.
A raised central fuselage hump with a dark canopy at its front. Two side-by-side engine nacelles at the rear of
the hump, with large round exhausts. Angular panel detailing and vent grilles on the wing. Four short spike guns
pointing forward from the wing roots and nacelles. A thin wing, cut square at the tips. Grey, worn.

The maintainer's reference sheet (a three-view orthographic plus perspective renders, "Babylon 5 Raider Fighter", and
the raider-base mood shot; kept in the maintainer's untracked `references/`) fixes the proportions the generator
follows: span about 2.4-2.7 times the length, leading edge about 54-56° from the centre line, a low wide centre body
(hump and two drums) whose engine exits sit nearly flush with the wing's trailing edge, thin square-cut tips, four
spikes hanging from the nacelle sides and wing roots. `docs/assets-raider.md` lists the numbers used (7.2 m × 17 m,
56°) and where they differ from the earlier written defaults (9 m × 17 m, 60°). Open: scale (is 17 m across right?)
and colour (worn grey-brown is used, per the mood shot); the maintainer can overrule both in `RaiderSpec`.

## Raider carrier (M12)

A blocky "battlewagon": a long rectangular box hull, dark green-grey with plating. **Two large concentric
rings** encircle the middle and aft of the box. Many cone- or spike-shaped launch pods (yellow-gold triangular
fins) stand around the rings, which gives it a spiky wheel look. Spindly antennae at the corners.
This is the first of two raider capital types.

## Raider Battle Wagon (second raider carrier type, M12)

A heavier raider carrier built around one huge ring, weathered grey-brown with heavy plating.

- **Core hull.** A long body of octagonal cross-section running fore-aft, tapering at the bow into a faceted
  cone. The cone ends in a small circular "eye" frame (an open ring) with two or three long, thin antenna spikes
  pointing forward.
- **Main ring.** One very thick ring encircles the hull just aft of mid-length: deep, with buttresses and panel
  bays around its rim, and its diameter about 1.3x the hull's length is wide. Seen end-on, the ring frames
  everything. Inside it, a cross-braced lattice (X-braces) connects the ring to the hull.
- **Launch tubes.** Four big tubes of hexagonal or octagonal section run fore-aft through the ring: one above,
  one below and one on each side of the core. Ribbed, with open fronts. Fighters (Delta-Vs) launch from these.
- **Stern.** A flat aft face with an engine block of **eight yellow-glowing nozzles** in two rows of four,
  between the upper and lower tubes.
- **Scale.** Several Delta-V lengths across the ring. A Delta-V beside it in the reference is tiny (roughly
  1/15 of the ring diameter).

## Raider base scene (mood reference)

A raider base station orbiting a Mars-like orange planet with a thin blue atmosphere limb. Delta-V fighters in
the foreground show **two bright yellow-white engine glows** at the rear of the central hump, worn brown-grey
plating and visible panel detail. Useful for raider colours, engine-glow colour and a planet backdrop.

## Maintenance bot (M7 ambience, tutorial set dressing)

A small craft a few metres across. Two large curved shell halves (blue-purple, with yellow-orange bands and a
painted number) sit side by side like a split egg or clam, joined by a grey central truss body. Two jointed
manipulator arms hang below with claw grippers. A sensor head with a green lens sits under the front centre.

## Civilian ships (M5)

Eight civilian hulls for free-flight traffic (Q5, Q8, Q11, Q16, D59). The maintainer supplied fan-made 3D-print models
of seven of them (STL files in the untracked `references/`, never committed); the Saturn 4 tanker is built from the
Saturn parts (Q16). As with the Starfury, nothing of them is copied.
`tools/refview` (M5-T11) turns a model into the ship frame, scales it to the length the design asks for and writes
orthographic views from six sides and two obliques, silhouettes, 24 to 32 cross-sections along the length, cuts along
the centre planes and a table of the model's separate shells into the gitignored `reports/refs/<ship>/`. Print models
are built from closed parts, so each pod, drum or tank is its own shell with its own box, which is where most numbers
below come from. The generators (T12 to T15) build the hulls from these words; `docs/assets-civilian.md` turns them
into parts, budgets and hardpoints.

### Saturn family: Earth Alliance freighters

The four Saturn STL models are one family built from the same parts. On screen this is the Earth Alliance freighter the
fan wiki calls the "Achilles" type: a disc-shaped bridge, a truss, a banded cylinder and cargo pods. The wiki notes
that these ships were assembled from a few reconfigurable parts, and that the tubular sections were first built for
the tanker in "Midnight on the Firing Line". "Saturn" is the fan models' name, not one from the show.

All four files share one frame: file +y is aft (disc at -y, engine at +y) and file z is up, so the mapping is
`--right=-x --up=+z --aft=+y`. Up is read from the captures: in "Achilles.jpg" and "Raiders01.jpg" the pods hang
below the banded cylinder. The models are mirror-symmetric, so the choice only decides which side is the top.

**The family is modular (Q16).** Every Saturn is one **lead ship** plus cargo. The lead ship is the same on all five
variants: the antenna, the command disc (the control section), the forward truss with its drum frame, and the engine
section. The cargo is one or two **cargo sections**, each **100 m long**: a transport section holds four pods on their
arms round a hull cylinder, a tank section three tanks. The Saturn 3 carries three of the same pods in a group
instead. A section is measured from half a pod gap ahead of its first pods to half a gap behind its last, so sections
laid end to end keep the pods evenly spaced, as the Saturn 8 model does. Each ship's length is the lead ship plus its
cargo:

**Scale.** With the Saturn 4 model at 100 m overall (T11's first reading), its four pods and one pod gap measure
63.10 m. Q16 makes that 100 m, so the family is 1.585 times larger: 0.09746 m per file unit of
`EA_Saturn_4_transport.stl`. Every size below is at that scale. The other models agree within a few per cent once
their lead ship is matched to the Saturn 4's. The tanker's tanks are then exactly the Saturn 4's hull cylinder and its
rows 96.9 m apart (we use 100 m, so its junction grows 3 m); the whole tanker model is 255.1 m long. The Saturn 8 model
is 266.2 m, its sections 103.5 m apart and its pods 30.8 x 15.8 x 43.6 m against the shared 31.1 x 17.3 x 43.0 m. The
Saturn 3 model, scaled the same way, is 91.1 m long without an engine.

**Texture (M5-T27, the `ea_civilian` pack).** Read from the same captures: the Saturns are clean, working paint, not
weathered hulks. Achilles.jpg and Red Star 9.jpg show long plates with fine dark seams on the disc and the cylinders, the
pods ribbed fore and aft with a light patchwork of greys; Epsilon.JPG shows the disc's rim and the bands sharp-edged and
evenly painted. So: long plates with thin dark seams and a few hatches, a light plate-to-plate tint, edges worn to dark
primer rather than bare metal, faint grime and short streaks aft of the seams, flat paint. The rim windows glow warm
white, the engine throat blue-white (Valjean.JPG). No markings or lettering beyond the bands.

No capture shows weapons, and the wiki lists "None" for the Achilles. The turrets below are Q14's, placed to cover the
hull's quarters. The drum frame's drums are the only turret-like mounts in the models; they stay RCS clusters. Every
variant has the lead ship's two turrets (Q32, 2026-11-07, replacing Q19's `dorsal_fwd` on the disc's top hub):
`flank_l` and `flank_r` on the disc rim's widest point at (∓14.43, 0, 19.1), up ∓x outboard, each on a slim post (0.8 m radius, the turret's base overhangs it) sunk 1.2 m into
the rim, so the command section is covered above, below and to both sides; the belly seen from astern stays a blind spot
on purpose. The cargo sections' turrets are dorsal (Q19): the ventral mounts first proposed (`ventral_fwd`, `ventral_aft`)
are dropped.

## Combat sounds (reference for the M5 sound pass)

The source is the YouTube clip "Starfury Dogfight with Raider Forces | Babylon 5"
(https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b3uTt3MGqPg), 1:12 to 1:53 only. It was measured locally from a mono 48 kHz downmix and
is not stored in the repository. The trimmed file and a `NOTES.txt` with every command sit in the maintainer's untracked
`references/sound/b5_dogfight/`. Nobody listened to it. Everything below comes from envelopes, spectrograms and spectra,
so it gives shapes and numbers for original synthesis in `tools/procgen/assets/sfx_combat.py`, never audio to copy. Times
are video times.

The mix is TV audio and heavily compressed, sitting between -11 and -15 dBFS RMS for all 41 s. Two layers dominate it,
and both were set aside.

- **Score.** A held low E (41, 62, 82, 123 and 165 Hz, an E with its fifth) runs from 1:12 to at least 1:48, with higher
  partials on a 330 Hz series. Its 20.6 Hz beat first looked like an event train. Confidence high.
- **Dialogue.** Radio chatter covers about half the window (1:12-1:18, 1:24-1:26.5, 1:29.5-1:32, 1:33.7-1:35.5,
  1:48-1:52). Its "s" sounds are 100-130 ms noise bursts at 3-10 kHz and look like effects. A burst counted as speech when
  the 250-2500 Hz voice band dropped 4-11 dB while it lasted. Effects leave the voice band alone. Confidence high.

The effects sit 6-10 dB under the dialogue and the score.

### Gun (one type found)

Four bursts at 1:29.54, 1:30.29, 1:31.16 and 1:32.04, each of three shots. A shot is a short click, then a cluster of
steady, bright, metallic tones held flat for about 100 ms until the next shot cuts in. The pitch does not move and there
is no low thump. The shots are near-identical: the main tone is 4.79 kHz in every one, to within 0.15 %. Confidence is
high that this is gunfire. The maintainer watched 1:29.5-1:32.0: it is the **Starfury's** gun (2026-10-25). No other
shot sound occurs in the window, so the raider gun, and how the two differ, is not measured.

### Roars and pass-bys (engine candidates)

Two shapes of broadband noise, neither with a voice-band dip or a low boom. Confidence is medium that they are ships
passing the camera, and low for which ship, or main engine versus manoeuvring jet.

- **Step roars** at 1:26.85 (held to 1:27.86), 1:28.03 and 1:44.75. The noise switches on in 5-35 ms, holds for up to a
  second with slow swells of about 3 dB and no decay, then stops within 30-50 ms. Starts and stops this hard are
  probably picture cuts. Inside the roar, bands of noise slide downward like a comb filter whose delay grows, the usual
  flanged pass-by. Read off the spectrograms, the bands fall about 1-7 octaves per second; an automated fit was
  inconclusive.

### Impacts and explosions (not found)

No sharp broadband event follows any gun burst. The 30-200 Hz band never rises more than 8.5 dB over its running median,
and then only for 40-200 ms inside speech or score phrases, so the window has no explosion booms. `hit_hull` (350 ms,
a 296 Hz inharmonic clank) and `explosion` (1.8 s, centroid 131 Hz) get no reference from this clip.

### Design notes for original synthesis

- **Gun.** Keep the character: constant pitch, a metallic inharmonic cluster from two-frequency FM, a click attack, a
  flat hold of about 100 ms, triplet bursts at about 9 shots per second. Against today's `pulse_fire` that means moving
  the energy up about three octaves (4-7 kHz instead of 0.5-1.3 kHz), dropping the falling sweep and the thump, and
  replacing the exponential decay with hold-and-cut. Choose our own base frequency and ratio near these values rather
  than reusing 2.06 and 0.67 kHz. A soft echo 60 ms after the burst is optional.
- **Raider gun.** No reference will be measured: the maintainer chose to invent it (2026-10-25). It should still sit
  with the Starfury gun as a pair, built the same way (click, flat hold, inharmonic cluster, hold-and-cut) but clearly
  another weapon, for example lower and buzzier as it is now, with a different burst rhythm.
- **Engines become per-thruster bursts.** The maintainer chose this model. Each main pod and each RCS jet sounds while
  it fires, from a voice placed where it sits on the ship, replacing the two global loops. Burst parameters suggested by
  the measurements:
  - Main pod: noise 400 Hz-16 kHz, peak 4-8 kHz, about -5 dB per octave above 1 kHz. Attack 5-35 ms; held while firing
    with a slow 3 dB wander at 0.5-2 Hz; release 30-50 ms on cutoff, up to 150 ms for a softer stop. Add a slow comb
    sweep (notches falling 1-4 octaves per second) for close passes. A body under 300 Hz is a design choice the
    reference neither confirms nor rules out; today's 36 Hz rumble could stay as a quieter layer.
  - RCS jet: noise 2-16 kHz, brightest at 6-16 kHz. Attack 25-100 ms; held for the firing time; release tau 120-250 ms
    for short pulses, up to 0.65 s after long burns. Brightness rises about an octave during the attack and falls during
    the release.
  - Today's `engine_loop` (nothing above about 260 Hz) and `thruster_loop` (90-900 Hz) sit 3-6 octaves below anything
    measured in the reference.
- **Hits and explosions.** No reference will be measured: the maintainer chose to invent them (2026-10-25), in keeping
  with the gun and engine sounds above (bright, metallic, short), while `explosion` keeps a low body for weight.
