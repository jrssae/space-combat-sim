# Milestone 7: Babylon 5 Station

> "The station." (`docs/roadmap.md`)

**Status:** **in progress** on branch `m7` (cut from `main` after M6 merged as WIP, tag `m6-wip`), started 2026-11-13. The maintainer answered Q1 to Q5 and Q7 to Q11 on 2026-11-13 (table below); Q6 is settled in T02 with the maintainer after T01. **The defence grid is not planned here**: where its emplacements sit and how its cannons and interceptors behave is a discussion between the maintainer and the controller (T02), and the plan's grid parts are filled in from it. This plan was written against `m5` at `5d81686` and assumes M6's plan (`m6-epsilon-space-at-true-scale.md`); tasks check M6 as built before relying on it.

Read `docs/architecture.md` first, especially §14.6 (launch bays and the mission framework), §14.7 (capital ships and stations),
§14.8 (the procedural asset framework), §17.11 (M4's interceptor stand-ins), §17.18 (the textured mesh pipeline), §18 (M5: the
`CINEMATIC` camera, missions, the front end, the native turrets of §18.10 and the big-hull handling of §18.11) and M6's §19 (the
moving frame, the ephemeris, scaled space and far representations). `docs/design-references.md` has the station's written
description and the maintenance bot's. Tasks are ordered inside each track; each is one PR-sized unit and names a recommended
agent model.

## Goal

Build Babylon 5: an 8 km station generated by code from the maintainer's references, its habitat drum turning, readable from a
fighter beside its bays and as a point from 1 000 km. It sits where M6's system data puts it, in the free-falling frame, with
compound collision a fighter can fly around and into its bays. It broadcasts to 1 000 km, so the AI sees it and a mission can
send raiders at it, and its defence grid fights them as the maintainer and the controller settle it. The player launches from a
Cobra bay through a cinematic launch, and the front end sits over a generated station interior instead of the bare sky. M8's
tutorial (launch, exterior tour, defend the station) is written on top of this.

## Scope

In:
- **References and measurements** of the station from Q1, written into `docs/design-references.md`.
- **The `babylon5_station` generator** replacing M4's `b5_station` stand-in: the frame (reactor, spine, hub and vanes, command
  section, bow) and the drum as separate pieces, textured meshes on M4's pipeline, near-field detail where Q2 asks for it,
  hardpoints (bays, docking aperture, defence-grid mounts from T02, lights, beacons, tour points).
- **A compound collision builder** in `tools/procgen/core` (convex pieces from parts), which M12's capital ships reuse.
- **The station in the game**: placed from M6's system data, kinematic in the free-falling frame, the drum turning as a moving
  body, hit volumes and rams, a `Targetable` in the `earth_alliance` faction, broadcasting to 1 000 km, section damage (Q5).
- **The defence grid** as T02 settles it, on M5's native turret rows.
- **Launch bays and the launch sequence**: a scenario can start "in launch bay N"; a cinematic launch camera; the hand-off.
- **The station interior** for the front end (Q7).
- **Traffic and ambience**: AI around an 8 km body, allies launching from the bays, civilian traffic (Q8), maintenance bots (Q11).
- A station sandbox scenario with tour points for M8's exterior tour.
- Console commands and providers for all of it (§6.4), `SettingsSchema` entries for every new setting, `info.md` kept current.

Out (explicitly):
- Landing and docking (Q4). Walking or flying inside the station beyond the bays.
- Destroying or disabling the station, blast damage (M12).
- The tutorial mission itself (M8). Voiced dialog (M15).
- Other stations and capital ships (M12).

## Design (to go into `docs/architecture.md` §20)

### D72 The station generator

- `tools/procgen/assets/babylon5_station.py` builds two pieces as `b5_station` does today: `frame` (stationary) and `drum`
  (turning about the station's long axis, its node centred on that axis). About 8 km along Z, bow at -Z, proportions from
  `docs/design-references.md` and Q1's references.
- Texel density in tiers, so an 8 km hull reads from 50 m (Q2) without one huge atlas: per-piece atlases for the broad paint and
  panels, and a shared detail layer (trim and panel maps tiled in world space) for the near field; T04 measures the VRAM.
- Hardpoints: each Cobra bay (opening, rail start and end, light), the docking aperture, the defence-grid mounts (T02's
  positions, as data), running lights and beacons, tour points (a name, a position and a view direction each), and the hull's
  damage sections (Q5).
- The output contract every asset meets (§14.8), plus the collision pieces (D73). `b5_station` and M4's `StationStandin` are
  replaced; the render comparison scenes switch to the new asset.

### D73 Compound collision from parts

- `tools/procgen/core/collision.py`: each part names its collision shape (box, capsule, convex hull of its own points), merged
  into a list of convex pieces per piece of the asset, with a budget (Jolt's piece count and each hull's point count) and a
  coverage test (every render triangle within a tolerance of a collision piece, and no piece far outside the render hull).
- The manifest gains a `collision.pieces` section; Godot builds one `StaticBody3D` or `AnimatableBody3D` per piece from it.
- The same builder serves the native hit volumes (spheres and boxes fitted to the same parts), so the hit sweep, the rams and
  the turrets' line-of-fire check (M5's Q33) all see the station's real shape.

### D74 The station in the game

- `src/world/station.gd`: the frame as a kinematic body and the drum as an `AnimatableBody3D` turned in `_physics_process` from
  sim time; spawned from the system data's station point (M6), moving with the frame as a test particle (M6, D65).
- The station is a `Targetable` (`earth_alliance`), a broadcaster at 1 000 km (M3-T17), and a big hull for the AI (M5's big-hull
  handling): avoidance, slashing passes and range measured to its nearest surface, not its centre.
- Section damage per Q5; the HUD's target box shows the station's sections.
- The station's lights compete for the scene's 8 light slots like any emitter (`LightPicker`), so its beacons do not starve the
  fight's muzzle flashes.

### D75 The defence grid

- **To be settled with the maintainer in T02** and written here: the emplacements' positions on the hull, the weapon kinds (the
  cannons and the interceptors), each kind's behaviour (what it targets, ranges, arcs, rates, how interceptors treat incoming
  fire), and the balance against raider waves.
- Fixed by the architecture whatever T02 decides: per-tick aim, gates and shots run in the native core on M5-T21's turret rows
  (`CLAUDE.md`'s language split), results identical with 1 and 6 threads; the station's own hull blocks its fire (Q33's rule);
  shots go through the projectile pool and `HitResolver`. M4's interceptor stand-ins (§17.11) are a starting point, not the
  behaviour.

### D76 Launch bays and the launch

- A scenario start `{"launch_bay": "cobra_bay_3"}` puts the player's ship on that bay's rail with its controls locked.
- `LaunchSequence` (`RefCounted`, pure): the rail's motion (accelerate along the rail from the bay's hardpoints), the shots of a
  `CINEMATIC` launch camera (M5's `CinematicShot`, keyed on the bay's hardpoints), and the hand-off: controls unlocked, the ship
  freed with the bay's velocity (Q3). Skippable with A or Start, as every cinematic.
- The mission API gets `launch_from(bay)`; wingmen launch from the next bays, a few seconds apart.

### D77 The front-end interior

- A generated interior (`tools/procgen/assets/b5_interior.py`, Q7) behind the menu, replacing M5's sky-only backdrop (M5's Q2),
  with its own lights and a slow camera; M8's front end goes from it into a launch.

### D78 Traffic and ambience

- Civilian traffic (Q8) uses M5's encounter director and civilian pool: arrivals through M6's gate, an approach to the docking
  aperture, and the ship leaving the world inside it (pooled again).
- Maintenance bots (Q11): a small generator and a mover with a few hull points each; ambient, so their per-tick motion is cheap
  enough to stay in GDScript only while their count is a handful (a number T11 records); more than that goes native.

### M7-T03 Compound collision builder

- **Goal:** the builder half of AC3 (D73). Early start.
- **Files:** `tools/procgen/core/collision.py`, the manifest's `collision.pieces`, its Python tests (budget, coverage, determinism),
  the Godot side that builds bodies from the section (`src/rendering/mesh_assets.gd` or a new loader), a GUT test on a small asset.
- **Notes:** start from one existing hull (a Saturn) so the builder is tested before the station exists; the native hit volumes
  come from the same parts.
- **Done when:** a test asset's pieces cover its render hull within tolerance and inside the budget, built in Godot.
- **Done (as built):**
  - **Builder:** `tools/procgen/core/collision.py`. A `Shape` is a box, a capsule (along its basis' z) or a hull (`box`, `capsule`,
    `hull`, `from_primitive` for a part's existing `parts.Collision`, `shape_for_part` for a part that does not pick: its own
    capsule if that holds its mesh within 0.5 m, else the hull of its points). `hull` keeps the corners that matter most (the
    axis extremes, then the point farthest outside the hull so far) up to the point budget. NumPy only, no new dependency.
  - **Budget** (`Budget`, the defaults): **128 shapes a piece, 32 points a hull**. Chosen for Jolt, not forced by it (Jolt refuses
    a hull above 256 points; cost per contact grows with both). `merge_to_budget` joins the pair whose common box adds the least
    empty space into one hull until the piece fits; `check_budget` raises on what is left over.
  - **Coverage:** `coverage(triangles, shapes, step)` probes every render triangle every `step` and returns `max_gap_m` (the
    render point farthest outside every shape) and `max_excess_m` (the shape point farthest from the render surface, read up to
    one `step` long), each with where; `Coverage.check(gap, excess)` raises. It is a test-time measure, not run in a build.
  - **Manifest:** `collision` = `max_shapes`, `max_hull_points`, `pieces[]`; a piece has `name`, `aabb_min`, `aabb_max`,
    `bound` (centre, radius), `shapes[]` and `hit_volumes[]`. A box or capsule entry reads exactly as a `hardpoints.json`
    collision entry; a hull is `points` in the piece's frame. `validate_collision_section` (`core/manifest.py`) refuses
    values that are not finite, unknown types and a piece over the section's own budget.
  - **Hit volumes:** one per shape, same order and name: a box or capsule is its own, a hull gets the tighter of its
    axis-aligned and principal-axes boxes. They are data only; nothing native reads them yet.
  - **Godot:** `src/world/collision_pieces.gd` (`CollisionPieces`, pure: `for_asset`, `from_manifest`, `piece(name)`; shapes as
    `BoxShape3D`, `CapsuleShape3D`, `ConvexPolygonShape3D`, hit volumes as `HitShape`s; a broken section is refused whole) and
    `src/world/collision_bodies.gd` (`CollisionBodies.build(piece, animatable)`: a `StaticBody3D` or `AnimatableBody3D` with a
    `CollisionShape3D` per shape; the caller sets layers and adds it).
  - **The Saturns** (`saturn_family`, generator version 3) write one piece, `hull`, a shape per part: 56 to 106 shapes (76 on
    the Saturn 4 transport: 75 hulls and the cargo cylinder's capsule), 51 to 86 kB of manifest. Against the drawn glb at a 1 m
    step: gap 0.73 to 0.75 m (the command disc's rim, a 28 m lens on 32 corners), excess 2.1 to 4.75 m (a hull across a concave
    strut set); the tests hold **gap <= 1.0 m, excess <= 5.0 m**. Merged to 48 shapes the Saturn 8 transport still passes
    (0.88 m, 4.75 m). The hit volumes: gap <= 0.15 m, excess <= 6.8 m (tests: 0.5 m, 7.5 m).
  - **The live game is unchanged:** ships still collide and take hits through `hardpoints.json`'s `collision[]`; no native code
    and no parity recording was touched. Putting 76 hulls on every Saturn would change contacts and cost, so it was not done.
  - **Tests:** `tools/procgen/tests/test_collision.py` (30: hulls, distances, the merge, coverage, the section, the manifest's
    refusals), four per Saturn variant and a tight-budget one in `test_saturn_family.py` (determinism is its existing
    byte-identical test, which now covers the section), `tests/unit/test_collision_pieces.gd` (10, with rays against a built
    body) on `tests/fixtures/collision_demo_manifest.json`; both new files are in the quick tier.
  - **For T04/T05:** call `col.section({"frame": [...], "drum": [...]})` with each piece's shapes in that piece's own frame (the
    drum's centred on its axis) and put it in the manifest's `extra`. A part that is concave (a bay mouth, the docking aperture)
    must be given as several shapes, or its hull closes the opening: the excess number shows it. Pick the station's step and
    tolerances there (a bay needs an excess well under a fighter's clearance) and run `coverage` in its tests; on a mesh that
    size keep `step` near the tolerance, since probes grow with area / step².
  - **For T06:** build bodies with `CollisionBodies.build`, set `CollisionLayers`, and feed `piece["hit_volumes"]` to
    `Damageable`/`HitResolver`, the rams and `set_turret_hull` (boxes and capsules, as today's 16-number volumes). The sweep's
