## Goal

Bring the setting's fleets into the game. Every capital warship in the maintainer's references is generated by code from
its reference model and show images, flies as a Newtonian body under the native core, and fights with its faction's
weapons. Capital ships take damage by sections, become disabled hulks or break up in stages, and every explosion becomes
a blast with distance falloff. The thirteen factions are data (D12.3), and the Shadows and Vorlons bring the
show's mechanics. M4's benchmark stand-ins go, replaced by the real ships. M10 also builds every other ship model in the
references (Q32): after it, only stations are left to model.

## Design (to go into a new section of `docs/architecture.md` when M10 starts)

### D12.1 Capital assets

- One generator per hull in `tools/procgen/assets/` (for example `ea_omega.py`), on a shared capital parts library in
  `tools/procgen/parts/` (hull sections, plating, weapon mounts, interceptor domes, launch bays and tubes, engine blocks,
  rotating sections, antennae) and a style per faction in `tools/procgen/styles/`.
- The output contract every asset meets (§14.8, `docs/assets-civilian.md`), plus:
  - **sections** (`manifest.sections[]`): a name, a kind (`reactor`, `magazine`, `fuel`, `bridge`, `hangar`, `engine`,
    `habitat`, `structure`), its share of hull points, its convex pieces and hit volumes, the hardpoints it holds, and
    for the explosive kinds a blast energy (D12.6);
  - **hardpoints** for every weapon kind (turrets, beam emitters, interceptors, missile launchers, mine layers), launch bays
    and tubes (a fighter's start pose and exit direction), engines (thrust direction and share), rotating parts, lights;
  - **collision** from M7's builder (D73), one set of convex pieces per section, so the break-up's chunks keep collision;
  - **break-up chunks** cut along the sections' boundaries on the hull's atlas (`core/chunks.py`).
- Texel tiers as M7's station (D72): per-piece atlases for the broad paint and a shared detail layer tiled in world space,
  so a 1 to 3 km hull reads from 50 m.
- Skins: a panel mosaic; Minbari and ISA hulls a hexagonal grid instead (Q45).
- Low and high texture sets per hull; far representations as M6's D70; a triangle and VRAM budget per class (Q38).

### D12.2 Organic hulls and sheen

- `tools/procgen/core/sdf.py` (signed distance fields, smooth unions, metaballs, spline-swept bodies, noise displacement)
  meshed to the textured mesh writer's input.
- A view-dependent sheen layer over the four maps, as the liveries are a layer: spherical-harmonic iridescence for the
  Vorlons, the Shadows' black hulls with their faint purple sheen and moving surface patterns; one shader include, its
  parameters per style. The Vorlons' faint bioluminescent glow is part of their sheen layer; Shadow
  and Vorlon organic parts have no seams or panel lines (Q46).
- Fully organic and seamless: the Shadows and Vorlons. Semi-organic, organic shapes with visible seams and panels (Q47):
  the Minbari, the ISA and the Drakh; T06's shapes carry the panel skin there (Minbari and ISA: the hexagonal grid).

### D12.3 Factions as data

- `data/factions.json` grows to `earth_alliance`, `renegades`, `raiders`, `narn`, `centauri`, `minbari`, `drazi`,
  `brakiri`, `vree`, `isa`, `shadows`, `vorlons`, `drakh` and `neutral`, with Q12's relations; scenarios override them.
- Renegades fly EA hulls with their own marking (a livery layer, as M4-T30b's wing liveries), chosen at spawn.
- A faction names its style, its fighters, its weapon kinds and its mine kind; hulls read them from data, not code.

### D12.4 Capital ships in the game

- A capital-scale `ShipSpec` (mass, inertia, thrust from the engine hardpoints, slow turn rates, `can_open_jump_point`,
  the sections) flown through the native flight step; a `RigidBody3D` with zero damping (`DAMP_MODE_REPLACE`), forces
  re-applied every tick; compound collision from the manifest; rotating sections as `AnimatableBody3D` (the Omega's drum).
- Hit volumes per section in the native core, so the hit sweep, the rams and the turrets' line of fire see the real
  shape; `Targetable` kind `CAPITAL`, broadcast range by class; the low-attention tier carries capitals and hulks as rows.
- Jump capability per class (Q22).
- Propulsion per hull (Q51): `reaction` hulls brake on retros where they have them, else flip and burn; `gravitic` hulls push
  along any axis at a fraction of their forward thrust and never flip. Broadcast range by class (Q51).

### D12.5 Sections and subsystems

- A hit lands on the section its hit volume belongs to (M4's `DamageZones`, M7's section damage). A section lost takes
  what it holds with it: its weapons stop, an engine section's thrust share is gone, a hangar's bays stop launching, a
  bridge lost cuts control, a reactor lost cuts power in stages.
- The ship is **disabled** (D12.7) when power or control is gone and no explosive section has blown; it is **destroyed**
  (D12.8) when a reactor, magazine or fuel section blows.
- Subsystems are targetable (Q17).
- **Rules (T03, Q53).** No armour. Sections share the hull by a template per role (docs/assets-capital.md, "Weapons and loadouts (T03)"). A magazine or fuel
  section at 0 blows (destroyed); a reactor at 0 shuts down (disabled) and blows after another 50 % of its points; a
  lost bridge releases the brain (last orders at half rate of fire), disabled only with the reactor or every engine
  gone too. Hits on a lost section pass half on to the nearest live structure. Mounts have their own points (light
  300 to beam emitter 2 500); a mount's damage counts against its section, and it dies with it.

### D12.6 The blast model

- One native kernel for every blast: mines, missiles, torpedoes, explosive sections and every ship's death. A blast has an
  energy and a radius; the damage to a hull falls smoothly to zero at the radius, measured from the blast's centre to the
  hull's nearest hit volume, and the same falloff gives the push on nearby bodies. T09 pins T03's curve in tests.
- A ship's death is a blast scaled by its class and what is aboard (Q18), replacing the placeholders M4 left on
  `HitInfo` (1e5 J a kilogram of hull, four bounding radii; §17.14) and the mines' flat 200 points. M4-T23's far flashes
  read the same energy.
- Run in the tick on the main thread like the hit sweep, identical with 1 and 6 threads.
- **Numbers (T03, Q53).** Each source has a centre damage `D0` and a radius `R` from a table by class and section kind
  (docs/assets-capital.md, "Weapons and loadouts (T03)"): falloff `(1 - (d/R)^2)^2`, damage to the nearest section; push `dv = I0 f(d) A / m`, capped at
  50 m/s; the flashes' energy `5e6 J x D0`. A fighter's death hurts within about 100 m (60 at the centre), a capital's
  reactor 4 500 out to 2.5 km, a dreadnought's 7 500 out to 3.5 km. The table lives in `data/capitals/capitals.json`;
  the manifest's `sections[].blast_energy_j` becomes a lookup by class and kind.

### D12.7 Disabled hulks

- A disabled ship loses power, control and thrust in stages, stops fighting and drifts on its last motion. Its brain is
  released; it stays `Targetable` and its sections can still be hit.
- In the tier it is a `FLAG_HULK` row (§17.14), never removed for being idle; Q19's cap scuttles the oldest.
- A jump point it held collapses (M6-T23's rule); an explosive section hit later blows it up (D12.8).

### D12.8 Staged break-up

- A capital's death is a plan, not one effect: secondary blasts walk from the explosive section outward, sections come
  apart as chunks on their own motion (M3's `StagedWreck` and `BreakupPlan`, generalised from the Starfury to sections),
  fires and venting, the reactor's blast last; each blast is a D12.6 blast and a far flash.
- Explosions pooled with shared meshes and materials (M4's review item L10).

### D12.10 Capital AI and carriers

Settled in T03 (Q16, Q17, Q53).

- **The `capital` brain** is native and runs in the AI job one tick behind like every brain, its commands applied at the
  join against the pilot's stamp. It absorbs T07's GDScript `CapitalHelm` (about 52 us a tick for one capital): the helm
  (reaction with retros or a flip; gravitic) runs every tick in Rust, the brain thinks at 5 Hz staggered; budget about
  8 us a tick a capital, threads identical, parity recordings for the helm.
- **States.** Approach; Engage: hold 0.7 x the main weapon's range and take the attitude that maximises mount dps times
  bearing (broadside for turret ships, bow-on for fixed bow guns, belly-on strafing passes for the Xill); Screen: an
  escort holds 0.8 to 1.2 km off its charge; Launch: a carrier launches on a hostile within 15 km and recalls before a
  jump; Withdraw at hull under 30 %, reactor under 50 % or every main engine lost: through its own point, else a friendly
  point within 20 km, else a gate, else it fights on (a disabled ship cannot); Hold on orders (the player's: M11).
- **Targets:** `threat x value / time_to_kill` with doctrine weights per faction (`capitals.json`). Anti-capital mounts
  take the brain's target; anti-fighter mounts pick their own on the turret rows, without the grid's "never first".
  Subsystems (Q17): heavy weapons aim at the hull's centre, at the target's mounts bearing on it when outgunned (enemy
  dps over 1.5x its own), at a withdrawing target's engines under the Centauri and Drazi "cripple" doctrine.
- **Fighters' attack runs:** approach along the direction least covered by the target's arcs (beams weighed most),
  re-planned every 2 s; fire from 1 500 to 400 m at a subsystem (mounts covering the approach, interceptors, engines,
  bays; torpedo craft the reactor), break off across the nearest beam arc, open to 3 km, repeat; abort at half the
  squadron lost, regroup at 5 km. Raiders never attack warships.
- **Carriers** launch their faction's fighters (Q53's counts) from their bays and tubes through the ship pool, replacing
  M4's stand-in launches; M7's launch sequence for AI ships is reused where it fits. Recovery into a bay is out.

### M10-T62 Section measure for open STLs (Q36)

- **Why:** a study on 2026-11-20 found that `section_mask` in `tools/refview/sections.py` leaks stripes on STLs that are
  not watertight (3 of the 7 surveyed, the Omega among them), so per-part section IoU cannot be trusted on those hulls.
- **Fix:** a robust inside test, for example a vote over several rays. A prototype is in
  `reports/stl_builder_study/lib.py` (gitignored scratch: read it, do not import it).
- **Tests:** Python tests on an open-shell fixture: a closed shape with faces removed gives the closed shape's mask.
- **Done when:** the tests pass and the sections already recorded (the station, the strike carrier) keep their scores.
- **Done (2026-11-20):** `section_mask` counts the winding along each pixel's row; where the row does not close (its crossings
  do not cancel: a gap in an open shell) it uses the pixel's column instead, and where neither closes it needs three of four
  rays (right, left, up, down) to call it solid. A watertight mesh closes everywhere, so it fills exactly as before. The sign of
  the winding is not used (the references' shells are not wound consistently; a sign test broke the station's planes). The
  API is unchanged. Tests: `test_refview_sections.py` (4: the closed drum, wall faces missing, faces missing on two sides, an
  open shell scored against its closed self; the last three fail on the old code). On the Omega (`EA Omega Destroyer.stl`,
  62 439 triangles) the stripes of 'inside' are gone and 19 planes take 0.17 s at 1 m a pixel (0.28 s at 0.5 m); picture in
  `references/screenshots/M10-T62 section measure/`. Recorded scores held: all 65 station planes (`test_station_fidelity.py`;
  scores equal to 4 places) except two exempt ones, which rise (cargo_bay_side 0.405 to 0.519, tower_x0 0.389 to 0.682; their notes in `station_fidelity.py` still quote the old numbers). The strike carrier has
  no recorded section compare; its masks equal the old ones on 15 z planes.
